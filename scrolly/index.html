ACT II · THE MIRAGE OF THE AVERAGE

“The Japanese don’t work more than Americans anymore.”

…That is what the official statistics say. Annual working hours: Japan 1,654, the United States 1,789 (2023) — Japan’s year is 135 hours shorter. Was everything above an illusion? No. The average blends two Japans into one number and dilutes them. The more short-hour workers join the workforce, the lower the average falls — and the overworked core disappears from view.

So stop blending. Count the share of workers putting in 49+ hours a week — and the ranking flips back.

One in five working men. For women, a share below America’s. What this extreme skew means inside the home — you will meet this asymmetry again in Act III.

Sources & notes

Source: ILO (JILPT Databook of International Labour Statistics 2025, Table 6-3). Share of employed persons usually working 49+ hours per week; the 22 countries with 2023 data. Annual working hours: Penn World Table (via Our World in Data, 2023) — a series separate from the OECD time-use diary.

APPENDIX · ABOUT THE DATA

These photographs were not all taken in the same year.

Time-use surveys are heavy instruments: thousands of people keep a diary of their day in 10-minute slices. Most countries run one only every 10–15 years, each on its own schedule. The 33 countries here are an album of "latest available snapshots", taken somewhere between 1998 and 2019. Touch a dot to see the country and its survey year.

Sources & notes

Japan's snapshot is from 2016 (based on the national Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities). Diary-based measurement — definitions differ from annual-hours labour statistics.
